How the counter is filled:
- Before 50 BC: "—" (no estimate for the Parisii town; the 25,000 for 59 BC in one Wikipedia mirror table has no clear basis and conflicts with the other mirror, so not used).
- 50 BC – AD 274: 6,000 → 8,000 (the scholarly range, rising), held at 8,000 in the 3rd century.
- AD 275 – 1327: "—". The two Wikipedia mirror tables disagree completely for 510–1300 (e.g. 1300: 228,000 vs 75,000), so they are not used.
- 1328 onwards: log-interpolated between the sourced anchors above; 1856→1860 follows the old limits, then jumps at the
  1 Jan 1860 annexation to the 1861 census (stated in the subtitle). 1817 skipped (mirrors give 713,966 vs 718,966).
- The 2024 Olympics and final segments hold 2,103,778 (latest official). INSEE's own 2024 estimate is 2,087,600 (Insee Flash
  Île-de-France no. 95, Oct 2024) — not used on screen because it is a provisional estimate.

## Simplifications and uncertainties

- Parisii: their town's site is disputed (Île de la Cité vs Nanterre). Shown as a few huts on the Cité and said so on screen
  ("where their town stood is debated"). The 52 BC burning is shown as a fire over those huts.
- Walls are ≈ traces through the gates/streets listed in the sources (Philip Augustus: gates Saint-Honoré, Montmartre,
  Saint-Denis, Saint-Martin, du Temple, Baudoyer; left bank Buci, Saint-Michel, Saint-Jacques, Bordet, Saint-Victor; ends at
  Tour du coin/Louvre, Tour Barbeau, Tour de Nesle, La Tournelle. Charles V: Tour du Bois, Place des Victoires, rue d'Aboukir,
  Grands Boulevards, Bastille, Tour de Billy. Farmers-General and Thiers along the boulevards named in the sources.)
  Removal: Philip Augustus walls shown gone by 1700 (≈; parts survive today, the source gives no demolition date);
  Charles V and Louis XIII walls removed 1670 (order of 6 June 1670); Farmers-General 1860; Thiers 1925 (≈ middle of
  1919–1929 demolition).
- Gallo-Roman wall: the eastern half of the Cité (City walls of Paris); line ≈; shown until 1200 (≈, no removal date found).
- Bridges: Petit Pont and Grand Pont/Pont au Change positions ≈ (the cardo crossed the island; no page coordinates opened);
  wood until 1200, then stone (≈ visual choice). Pont Neuf from its coordinate. Later bridges not drawn.
- Roads: cardo ≈ along rue Saint-Jacques; Grands Boulevards from 1671 ≈ along the Charles V / Louis XIII line;
  Champs-Élysées drawn only from 1836 (no date opened for the avenue itself); Rivoli/Sébastopol from 1855 (Haussmann);
  bd Saint-Michel from 1859 (≈); railway to Saint-Germain ≈ (line drawn north-west from the Europe quarter, 1837);
  Périphérique ≈ 4.5 % outside the Thiers line, from 1973.
- Districts are ≈ areas for building symbols, not surveyed boundaries: Roman left bank (~100 ha) and island; late-Roman
  Cité; right bank 10th–12th c.; inside the Philip Augustus and Charles V walls; left-bank faubourgs; west right bank;
  Île Saint-Louis (≈ 17th c., no date opened); 18th-c. faubourgs inside the Farmers-General line; Haussmann rebuilding
  1853–1872 (Marais kept); annexed communes 1860–1914; inner/outer suburbs; La Défense 1964–2015.
- Landmark sizes from the sources where given (Notre-Dame 128 × 48 m with 69 m towers, Louvre castle, Bastille, Tuileries
  façade, Place des Vosges, Invalides dome, Arc, Garnier, Sacré-Cœur, Eiffel, Montparnasse, Pompidou, Grande Arche, forum,
  arena); Sainte-Chapelle, Hôtel de Ville and Cluny baths are modest ≈ sizes. Louvre Palace drawn as one courtyard block
  from 1546 (≈; Francis I's works from 1528, the castle demolished 1528–1660). Arena kept as a ruin until 600 (≈; it was
  used again in 577 and filled in after c. 1210).
- Notre-Dame: drawn rising 1163 → 1260s; after 15 Apr 2019 without spire and with a dark roof; restored from Dec 2024.
- Fires: 52 BC (island huts), May 1871 (Tuileries/rue de Rivoli block; Hôtel de Ville) — areas ≈. Smoke only for 845, 856,
  the Bastille 1789, the January 1871 shelling (left bank) and Notre-Dame 2019.
- Camps ≈: Vikings on both banks 885–886 (sites not given in sources); German siege lines 1870–71 drawn 9–12 km out
  (the source gives the forts 2–5 km outside the wall; the camp positions are illustrative).
- Modern banks of the Seine are used throughout (the island shapes and quays changed over time; not modelled).
