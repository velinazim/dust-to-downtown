# Istanbul — research notes

Episode: ~15 min, 667 BC → 2026. Every on-screen date/number below has its source (all pages opened during research,
3 Nov 2026; full list in `_sources.json` / city.json `sources`). Abbreviations: TL = Timeline of Istanbul (WP),
HI = History of Istanbul (WP), CP = Constantinople (WP), WC = Walls of Constantinople (WP), BYZ = Byzantium (WP),
FoC = Fall of Constantinople (WP), BRIT = Britannica "Istanbul: Centuries of growth", IT-NÜF = İstanbul Tarihi,
"İstanbul nüfusu: Fetihten XVIII. yüzyıl sonlarına kadar" (PDF), STAT = Istanbul region (statistical) census table (WP).
Landmark coordinates are the published coordinates of each page (Wikipedia infobox unless noted). Wall courses,
district outlines, fire and camp areas are simplified traces between known points (marked ≈); expect errors of
~100–300 m. OpenStreetMap is not reachable from the Claude session, so traces were drawn by hand and checked against
the preview map (`preview/map_core.png`); sea walls and wall ends are snapped to the real shoreline from geo.json.

## Population on screen

No figure (counter "—") before AD 193 and from 626 to 1142 (scholars' figures for the 7th–11th centuries disagree too
widely: CP gives 500,000–800,000 for the 9th–10th c. after Chandler, while Istanbul (WP) speaks of a fall from half a
million; nothing reliable to show).

Anchors (log-interpolated between them): 193 15,000 (TL); 324 20,000 (TL); 368 150,000 (TL); 393 200,000 (TL);
540 500,000 (CP: about 500,000 under Justinian; TL 300,000–500,000 for 537); 545 350,000 (**interpolation**: CP's
20–40 % plague deaths applied to 500,000 → 300,000–400,000, midpoint); 1150–1203 400,000 (CP: mid-12th c. and
Talbot's figure before 1204); 1261 35,000 (TL, CP); 1282 70,000 (CP, end of Michael VIII's reign); 1348 80,000 (TL);
1453 50,000 (CP; TL 40,000–50,000; IT-NÜF 50,000–60,000 before the siege); 1481 80,000 (HI; BRIT 60,000–70,000
c. 1480; IT-NÜF: 1477 estimates from 60,000 to 98,000); 1535 350,000 (IT-NÜF, Barkan); 1600 400,000 and 1690 400,000
(IT-NÜF: Mols 200,000–400,000 for the late 16th/early 17th c. and "over 400,000" for the late 17th; Barkan/Mantran's
700,000 for 1571–80 is the high end); 1800 500,000 (HI gives 400,000 in 1800, Istanbul (WP) ~570,000 by the end of
the 18th c., Demographics (WP, Chandler) 500,000 in 1815 → middle value); 1860 715,000, 1890 874,000, 1900 942,900
(Demographics of Istanbul, Chandler; the page's table is garbled, pairing year↔value is inferred); 1914 1,125,000
(TL, HI); 1927 onward: census figures for the **province** (STAT): 1927 794,444; 1935 883,599; 1940 991,237;
1945 1,078,399; 1950 1,166,477; 1955 1,533,822; 1960 1,882,092; 1965 2,293,823; 1970 3,019,032; 1975 3,904,588;
1980 4,741,890; 1985 5,842,985; 1990 7,309,190; 1997 9,198,809; 2000 10,018,735; 2007 12,573,836; 2010 13,255,685;
2013 14,160,467; 2016 14,804,116; 2018 15,067,724; 2020 15,462,452; 2022 15,907,951; 2024 15,701,602;
2025 15,754,053 (TÜİK, 31 Dec 2025; also held for 2026, the latest official figure).
**Notes:** pre-1927 figures are city estimates, from 1927 the province (the 1927 urban figure was 695,813; İT
"Cumhuriyet sonrası" gives 691,000 within the municipal limits). The 1914→1927 drop is drawn as a smooth fall
(HI says about 500,000 in 1924, Chandler 817,000 in 1925 — disputed, so no 1924 anchor).

## Left out / simplified (uncertain)

- Exact founding year of Byzantion (sources 667–619 BC): subtitle says "7th century BC" only.
- Constantine's 8 Nov 324 rename / 4 Nov 328 dedication (TL) vs CP's 324/330: on screen 324 and 11 May 330 (CP).
- Haydarpaşa (1908), Galata Bridge generations 1863/1912 (drawn as one iron bridge 1875–1992), Tünel line,
  the Byzantine harbours and Yenikapı excavations, Great Palace, Hagia Irene, Basilica Cistern: not drawn (no
  footprint/coordinates opened, or too small).
- 1955 pogrom, 1999 earthquake (Izmit, BRIT: >15,000 dead "near Istanbul"), 2013 Gezi protests: left out to keep the
  focus on how the city grew; dates are in the sources if wanted later.
- Land reclamation at Yenikapı (2010s) and the airport's site are shown as today's land throughout.
- Population of the Ottoman 16th–18th centuries is very uncertain (200,000–700,000); see anchors above.
- Yenikapı: the modern shore there is a 2010s landfill. It is drawn as water until 2013 (timed_water fill, outline ≈ =
  today's land south of the old shore trace; the year is a visual approximation tied to the Marmaray segment, never on
  screen), and the Marmara sea wall follows a hand trace of the old shore (≈) there instead of the modern one.
- Bridge ends are found by walking from each bridge's published centre along its direction (≈ bearing) to the first
  land ≥ 30 m from the shore in geo.json. The YSS bridge is shown from the start of 2016 (opened 26 Aug 2016).
- Lakes and reservoirs come from OpenStreetMap and are shown from the start (several are modern reservoirs).
